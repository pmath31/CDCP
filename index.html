1. The promise

A program built to improve access to care

The Canadian Dental Care Plan exists to help eligible Canadians access dental care they may otherwise struggle to afford. That is the visible public promise.

The less visible reality is that access depends on the machinery behind the program: eligibility review, evidence handling, exception management, and the ability to keep service delivery working over time. Official statistics reported 6,395,559 approved applicants for the 2025 to 2026 benefit year as of February 28, 2026.

Access is the promise. Delivery is how that promise is kept.
2. The delivery reality

National benefits are easy to announce. Administering them well is much harder.

A national benefit program requires more than an application form and a website. It needs the ability to validate eligibility, manage documents and evidence, route and resolve cases, support reassessments, and prepare for renewals at scale.

This is where many executive narratives go soft. They talk about the launch and skip the administration problem. That misses the real work.

The real challenge is not launch. It is administration at scale.
3. Why the capability was needed

Without the right systems, scale creates friction, cost, and risk

As the program grows, administration becomes more complex. Without the right digital and operational capabilities, the program faces increasing pressure from manual case handling, fragmented evidence review, disconnected data sources, and rising renewal and reassessment demand.

At small scale, those problems create friction. At national scale, they create operational risk.

The business case is not modernization for its own sake. It is the ability to administer a national benefit responsibly.
4. Concrete before / after

From manual Excel tracking to scalable case management

At one stage, the team had to manage approximately 100,000 cases manually using Excel-based processes. That may support immediate operations when a program is ramping up. It is not a durable operating model for a national benefit.

Manual coordination is not a scale strategy.
5. What is being built

A digital case-management foundation for delivery and integrity

The program needs more than front-end intake. It needs a structured operating backbone. The case-management platform is intended to support workflow management, evidence processing, review and reassessment support, exception handling, and program integrity.

Calling this “just a CRM” would undersell it. The more accurate description is a control and operations platform that helps the program verify eligibility and support more consistent decisions at scale.

This is not a front-end project. It is a control and operations platform.

9. Capability maturity

The capability is being built in stages

FoundationFoundational case-management and MER support
ExpansionExpanded workflows, automation, and integration capability
ScaleReadiness for higher-volume processing, renewals, and broader automation

The point is not simply that work is underway. The point is that the capability is maturing from manual support toward a more scalable and durable operating model.

The goal is not just to deploy capability. It is to mature it into something sustainable.
10. Business outcomes

The same scene now shifts to outcomes instead of inputs

These capabilities are intended to support more consistent decisions, reduce avoidable manual handling, improve responsiveness, and help ensure benefits go to eligible Canadians. Official statistics reported 4,043,306 unique approved applicants had received care since launch as of February 28, 2026.

This is where the visual shifts attention away from the pipes and toward what the operating model is meant to achieve.

Technology matters here only because it changes operational outcomes.
11. The hard truth

Keep the same scene, but change the overlay to future strain

The next phase is not about celebrating that a solution exists. It is about ensuring the platform is ready for higher case volumes, renewals, operational onboarding, partner coordination, and the remaining automation needed to reduce risk and manual burden.

This overlay keeps the architecture visible, but changes the data cards so the audience sees where future pressure lands.

Launch is not the finish line. Sustainable administration is.
12. The next frontier

Only after the operating foundation is visible should the AI layer appear

A discovery session is planned to identify where AI can improve business outcomes as the platform matures. The focus should stay on operational pain points and program outcomes, not AI for its own sake. The goal is not simply to digitize administration. It is to build a smarter operating model over time.
