1. The promise

Start with Canadians, not systems

The Canadian Dental Care Plan exists to help eligible Canadians access dental care they may otherwise struggle to afford.

Official statistics reported 6,395,559 approved applicants for the 2025 to 2026 benefit year as of February 28, 2026. That is the visible public promise.

The story should open with human value, then peel back to reveal the machinery underneath.
2. The delivery reality

The hidden machinery begins to emerge

A national benefit program needs more than intake. It needs validation, evidence handling, case routing, exception management, and renewals at scale.

This is the pivot point where the visual starts to reveal the operating model instead of replacing it with a new graphic.

National benefits are easy to announce. Administering them well is much harder.
3. Why the capability was needed

Scale turns process gaps into operational risk

Without the right digital and operational capabilities, the program faces growing strain from manual case handling, fragmented evidence review, disconnected data, and rising renewal pressure.

The business case is not modernization for its own sake. It is a more durable operating model.

At small scale these are frictions. At national scale they become risks.
4. Concrete before and after

Show the spreadsheet burden collapse into structure

At one stage, the team had to manage approximately 100,000 cases manually using Excel-based processes.

In this scene, spreadsheet fragments flood the canvas, then collapse toward a structured operating backbone instead of forcing the audience to jump to a new page concept.

Manual coordination is not a scale strategy.
5. The platform

The hub becomes the focal point

Now the audience has context. This is where Health Canada’s case-management and control platform becomes the core visual object.

It is not “just a CRM.” It is the operating backbone for review, orchestration, traceability, and integrity support.

This is not a front-end project. It is a control and operations platform.
6. Pipeline overlay: ESDC

Keep the scene fixed. Turn on only the ESDC story.

The same architecture stays pinned. Only the ESDC path lights up, along with overlay boxes and timing cards.

Replace the placeholders with real cadence and record volumes when you are ready.

This is where to show timing, records per cycle, and the business event each feed supports.
7. Pipeline overlay: PSPC

Same canvas. New emphasis.

Now the overlay switches to PSPC. The scene does not reset, which makes the mail and document flow easier to absorb.

This is the moment to show inbound and outbound mail, document packages, turnaround windows, and operational role.

The audience should not have to relearn the diagram every time the story moves.
8. Pipeline overlay: CRA

Then isolate validation and mismatch handling

The visual now highlights CRA while keeping the rest of the architecture stable in the background.

Official statistics also reported 27,968 participating oral health providers as of February 28, 2026. That is one reason validation and exception handling matter at scale.

Show validation timing, reviewed records, and how mismatches become operational follow-up.
9. Outcomes

Shift the emphasis from pipes to results

Official statistics reported 4,043,306 unique approved applicants had received care since launch as of February 28, 2026.

The same architecture now pushes attention toward consistency, responsiveness, traceability, and program integrity.

Better systems are not the outcome. Better delivery is.
10. The hard truth

Use the same scene to show future strain

The next challenge is scale: higher case volumes, renewals, operational onboarding, partner coordination, and the remaining automation needed to reduce manual burden.

The overlay changes again, but the operating model stays visible so the audience sees exactly where pressure lands.

Launch is not the finish line. Sustainable administration is.
11. The next frontier

Add the intelligence layer only after the foundation is clear

A discovery session is planned to explore AI opportunities tied to business outcomes: OCR for member-submitted documents, anomaly and fraud detection, seasonality analysis, and predictive analytics.

The current public page notes renewals for the 2026 to 2027 benefit year run from April 15 to June 1, 2026.

The goal is not AI theatre. It is more intelligent operational support over time.
